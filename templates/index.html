Taking a good photo

  • Good light. Use daylight and avoid flash glare.
  • Close and sharp. Fill the frame with the spot and hold still.
  • Clear skin. Move hair aside and remove marks or stickers.
  • One spot per photo. Scan each spot separately.

Understanding the result

  • Affected. The spot shows features linked to skin cancer. Book a dermatologist visit soon.
  • Not affected. No concerning features found. Keep watching for changes in size, color or shape.

Future Work

Planned improvements to make DermaScan more reliable and transparent.

Grad-CAM Visual Explanations Implemented

After each scan the page shows your photo alongside a heatmap highlighting the region the model focused on most. Warm colours (red/yellow) mark the highest-attention areas. This supports interpretation but does not prove the model reasoned correctly — the model may focus on hair or image artefacts rather than the lesion itself, as noted in the original paper (Figs. 7–8).

Calibrated Confidence Implemented

Raw neural-network probabilities are adjusted with temperature scaling so that a reported 90% confidence reflects roughly 90% real-world accuracy. Every scan returns a single decision — affected or not affected — against a 0.5 threshold on that calibrated probability, rather than an uncalibrated raw score.

Image-Quality Warning Implemented

Every scan is screened for blur, low light and overexposure before the result is shown. When a problem is detected, a notice appears next to your photo and beside the result, so you can retake the shot if needed. The check is advisory only: a usable photo is never blocked, and the prediction is always returned.

Lesion-Level Data Split & External Validation Planned

The HAM10000 dataset contains multiple images of the same lesion. Splitting by lesion ID rather than by image prevents data leakage and gives a more honest accuracy estimate. Validating on a fully independent dataset (e.g. ISIC 2019) would further confirm whether the model generalises beyond its training distribution.

Transformer-Based Architecture Planned

Vision Transformers (ViT) and hybrid CNN-Transformer models have shown improved performance on dermoscopy benchmarks. Replacing or augmenting MobileNetV2 with a pre-trained ViT could improve sensitivity on rare lesion types without requiring additional labelled data.

Multi-Class Classification Planned

The current model outputs a single malignant/benign score. Extending it to predict all seven HAM10000 classes (melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, actinic keratosis, etc.) would give clinicians more specific information and allow the heatmap to highlight class-discriminative features per lesion type.
